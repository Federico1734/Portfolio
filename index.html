Narrative Design

World-building · Lore Systems · Branching Structures

What I bring

Narrative design matters more than most realize, often more than the writing itself. A world too large for its game overwhelms the team and the players equally: balance and intentionality count more than volume. I approach it as a player first, asking how the lore lands before I build it. I write all of it, documentation and text, at the intersection of narrative and game design.

Key points
  • Lore systems architecture: structured, layered documentation that developers can build from
  • Narrative as mechanics: world logic that generates and constrains gameplay systems, not just flavor
  • Branching narrative structure: multi-path flowcharts where player agency feels intentional
  • Narrative that lands: world elements designed to be felt, not just filed away in a codex

Game Writing

Dialogue · NPC Voices · Environmental Storytelling

What I bring

Good game writing doesn’t announce itself. It works through what characters don’t say as much as what they do, through choices that feel personal, and through lines written to land in context, not just on the page. I test every line against how it plays: does it serve the scene, the character, and the moment?

Key points
  • Character voice built into every line: vocabulary, rhythm, and what gets left unsaid
  • NPC voice design: distinct speech patterns, vocabulary, and emotional register per character
  • Branching conversations where player choices land differently on replay
  • Performance writing: voice direction, stage notes, and barks embedded in the script itself

Game Design

GDD Authoring · Systems Design · Production Documentation

What I bring

Game design lives or dies in the gap between concept and implementation. A great idea that can’t be documented clearly, balanced, or built by a team isn’t a design: it’s a note. I write full GDDs that hold together from the first mechanic to the final balance pass, structured so that any developer can pick them up and build.

Key points
  • Full GDD authoring: concept to production-ready spec, structured for developers not just designers
  • Systems thinking: mechanics, economy, and progression designed as a coherent whole
  • Balance documentation: damage curves, progression pacing, competitive viability across roles
  • Core loop design and mock-up creation: concept validated on paper before production begins

Quest Design

Branching Structures · Moral Choices · Player-Driven Outcomes

What I bring

A quest is a promise: that the time spent will matter and the world will feel real. I design quest structures that fit the game they serve, whether that means branching paths, tightly scripted beats, or something in between. The goal is always the same: every step gives the player something worth experiencing, and the structure never gets in the way of that.

Key points
  • Multi-path flowcharts with every branch earned by player action, not randomness
  • Quests that reveal who the player is, who the NPCs are, and what the world holds, not just what gets unlocked
  • Multiple-act pacing applied to individual quest arcs, with a solid base that can be expanded: setup, complication, resolution
  • World-state integration: quests that shift meaning based on what the player already knows

Scriptwriting

Voiceover · Cutscenes · Cinematic Dialogue

What I bring

A script is not just text: it’s instructions for a performance. Line breaks, emphasis, tone shifts, pauses: all of that lives in the writing before it reaches the director or the actor. I write scripts that communicate intent clearly, so the performance begins on the page and arrives intact on screen.

Key points
  • VO scripts built for production: pacing, breath marks, and emphasis in the text itself
  • Cutscene structure: visual storytelling through dialogue and stage direction, not just words
  • Subtext and economy: every line pulls double duty, character plus plot, or character plus world
  • Format discipline: production-ready scripts that hand off cleanly to directors and voice actors

Localization (IT/EN/ES)

Italian · English · Spanish · Cultural Adaptation

What I bring

Localization fails when it stops at translation. A joke that doesn’t land, a tone that shifts between scenes, a character who sounds different in every language: these are failures of adaptation, not vocabulary. I work in three languages natively and approach every text the same way: what does this need to feel like, and how do I make it feel that way here?

Key points
  • Idiomatic adaptation: preserving tone, humor, and emotional register, not just meaning
  • Character voice across languages (IT/EN/ES): speech patterns and personality intact after translation
  • Cultural mediation: knowing what reads naturally vs. what needs reframing per market
  • Multilingual consistency: catching tone drift, false cognates, and register mismatches

Professional Test

Larian Studios reached out directly and sent this test as part of their hiring process for a writing position. The assessment ran over two weeks. I made the shortlist. They ultimately went with another candidate, but this is what I submitted.

The Brief

The test brief: create a memorable NPC encounter that demands a genuine choice, not a combat encounter, but a dialogue that reveals who the player really is.

Revrykal: The Varsh of Death

Revrykal is not death itself: he is its caretaker. Ancient, imprisoned in a forgotten ruin, awakened by the player breaking through a collapsing wall. Dignified, melancholic, and alien in his perspective on mortality.

The Five Endings
  • Fight: Player attacks — defeated effortlessly, then spared and released, unimpressed
  • Diplomatic Reveal: Patient investigation, learns Revrykal’s true identity
  • Accept Responsibility: “Yes, I freed you, I don’t regret it” — an alliance is offered
  • Denial: “This wasn’t my doing” — a blood-mark left as a promise
  • Fate: “Maybe you’re the treasure I was after” — player becomes part of his mythology

First-Person Psychological Adventure · Unreal Engine 5

Genre

Platform
PC

The Project

Hungerless is a first-person psychological adventure set in a surreal, obscure atmosphere. Protagonist: Victoria, isolated in her apartment, unemployed, tormented by Bulimia Nervosa (an eating disorder) and by a spectral presence called the Mistress. The Mistress is not a villain. She is a mirror.

Players inhabit Victoria’s distorted perception: controlling food and emotions, navigating denial, using a smartphone as both narrative tool and puzzle element. Environmental design shifts with her psychological state.

Gameplay Design
  • Core mechanics built around ED symptoms as gameplay systems: control, denial, distorted self-image
  • Smartphone integration — web searches and chat messages as narrative and puzzle elements
  • Environmental puzzles that change with Victoria’s psychological state
  • First-person perspective reinforces the claustrophobic, subjective experience
Recognition

Exhibited with a dedicated stand at Romics, Italy’s largest gaming and comics convention. Also 3rd place at Zagarolo Game House – IV Call.

Development Status

After Romics, the team and I went separate ways. The project is continuing development independently under Bastet Paw. Due to an NDA, I cannot share further materials — including the trailer shown at the Romics stand. You can follow the project’s ongoing development at the link below.

A fast-paced sidescrolling action roguelite fusing two mythological hells: Dante’s Inferno from the Divine Comedy and Naraku, the labyrinthine hell of Japanese mythology. The protagonist D. descends through Naraku in search of his kidnapped beloved, aided by Beatrice and tempted by Lucifer’s sins. The deeper he goes, the more he sins, the worse his ultimate fate. High difficulty, run ’n gun combat, and a Karma system that makes moral choices mechanically consequential.

Italy–Japan Collaboration

A cross-cultural co-production between Vigamus Academy (Rome) and Vantan Academy (Tokyo), supervised by Suda51, creator of Killer7, No More Heroes, Shadows of the Damned. I served as the creative communication bridge: aligning narrative concepts and mediating cultural differences in game design philosophy.

The project was developed within Vigamus Academy’s professional game design program. Following a team restructuring mid-development, I assumed the Lead Game & Narrative Design role, stepping in to take ownership of the creative direction and drive the project to completion.

Systems Designed
  • Karma system: every action shifts karma on a white-to-red gauge, affecting the final boss
  • Seven Deadly Sins as power-ups — altar pickups providing tactical advantages at a narrative cost
  • Holy Machinarium: real-time in-motion skill tree using souls from enemies
  • Diary/Datalog: persistent lore entries unlocked through exploration

The Story

Nyck is a gruff, solitary private detective. A missing girl case pulls her into a mystery that spirals beyond anything ordinary, leading to the hidden walls of an opera house, where a kidnapper uses elaborate traps to make the search seem supernatural. The Concept

Tommy, an 11-year-old dreaming of becoming a guitarist, enters a world where adults have lost all color and joy. His guitar lights everything up. Adults appear grey and uniform; Tommy’s world responds to his creativity and music.

The game captures the dualism between adult rationality and childhood instinct: monochrome vs. multicolor, programming vs. discovery, malaise vs. wonder.

  • Character arcs and emotional beats compressed into 72 hours
  • Game design on puzzle structure, tying music mechanics to emotional progression

Overview

An original MMORPG 3D space simulator with 6 civilizations, each in its own galaxy. Players choose a civilization, build their fleet, and compete in high-speed PvP and PvE battles. Inspired by Fractured Space. Full GDD and balance document delivered as a freelance commission in approximately one month.

Core Systems
  • Ship combat: 6-sided hull system — directional damage forces tactical positioning
  • 3 ship classes: Heavy, Medium, Light — each with distinct playstyle and economy role
  • 6 civilization galaxies with capital stations, planetary sectors, wormhole travel
  • Economy: resources, crystals, ore mining, crafting, player-owned cities and bays
  • Reputation system and rotating PvE events alongside ranked PvP
Balance Document

A dedicated balance document covers weapon damage curves, class matchup tuning, resource acquisition rates, and progression pacing — ensuring competitive viability across ship types from launch.
